Question text

The concept of growth models doesn't just apply to modern tech start-ups and scale-ups.  The image below was drawn by Walt Disney in 1957 to describe how Disney Corporation worked.

More recently, Disney launched the Disney+ streaming service.  Using your knowledge of this service and any available material you can find on the internet, describe the growth model for Disney+.

Answer text Question 1
Feedback

    An Aggregator Effect:  Disney+ is the only streaming service where all Disney assets can be viewed, attracting viewers who like Disney movies and programmes. The more content that is created by Disney, the stronger this effect becomes.
    CGM: Users pay subscription->Disney buys ads with revenue->Some users sign-up and pay subscription.  Disney likely "spiking the loop" with extra expenditure on paid ads/linear-marketing to bring critical mass of users to service, thereby seeding other CGMs.
    CGM: Organic, Direct CGM: Viewers pay subscription->Disney uses money to make new content-> Users watch new content->tell friends by word-of-mouth->some sign-up and pay subscription. 
    CGM: Content-based, indirect CGM: Users can share account with up to seven other users, essentially sharing content. But only four devices can be active simultaneously. Eventually, this creates a pressure for some users to sign-up for their own account.
    Activation - managing activation inequality:  Users can sign-up for free for a period of time, thereby demonstrating value prior to requiring users to pay for the service.
    Engagement Intensity - Regular new content - movies and box sets released.   Group watch feature to encourage groups of people to watch together.  In-app recommendation engine.   In-app auto-start on next episode.   Regular emails to signed-up users with recommendations on related content, and new content.  Multiple user profiles on the same account ensure that recommendations are relevant to individual users.

(Marking:  1 mark for any 5 of these, up to a maximum of 5 marks. Similar content and mechanisms not covered here will be considered for marks too, if reasonable).

Question text

Swipii is an app-based start-up that enables shoppers to receive cash-back when they make purchases in shops.   New users  first link their debit cards with Swipii then they sign-up.  Afterwards, whenever they shop in a store/cafe/restaurant etc. that has  also signed-up for Swipii, those users will receive "cashback" into their Swipii account. Businesses can also offer special offers, for example 10% cashback on a coffee purchased before 10:00am on a Tuesday, to encourage customer to visit.  So, in effect, Swipii is a two-sided network of shoppers and shops.  

When Swipii enters a new country, which is the best strategy: to target some shops etc. across as many cities in the country as possible, or to focus on just one or two cities at a time?  Explain your answer.
Answer text Question 2
Feedback

Swipii should concentrate on one or two cities at a time.   There are two main reasons:

    Referral mechanisms will work better when users get to a critical mass. This is more likely when Swipii's firepower is concentrated in one city.
    Swipii is a two-sided network with corresponding network effects - It's easier to start with a small network in one city, for example, because it is then easier to create an Atomic Network.  Once this is established, and the product is proven, it is easier to expand.

(1 mark for each of these points)  

Credit would also be given for saying: in a new market, it is important to establish product-market fit (which may exist in a new market to the same extent as existing markets).  Focussing on one city makes it easier to learn and test user experience quickly.

Question text

An early stage start-up acquires 1500 customers a month on average, at an average CPA of $80.    The start-up uses a sales team to convert customer leads to revenue-generating customers, at a total salary cost of $120000 per year. The start-up estimates its customer 6-month LTV to be $300.

Comment on the company's channel acquisition strategy.  
Answer text Question 3
Feedback

It's first necessary to calculate the LTV:CAC ratio.

6-month LTV = $300.

Total Channel Acquisition Costs per customer =  $80

Additional costs to convert customers to revenue = $120000/(12*1500) = $6.67   (we've converted yearly total cost to monthly average per customer).

Total costs to acquire each customer = $86.67

Therefore LTV:CAC = 300:86.67 = 3.46   (3 marks for calculation)

Then we need to decide whether this ratio is generally acceptable.  We can see, from the above, that the ratio >3:1. This means that the acquisition itself is profitable. The additional headroom in the ratio means that the acquisition process is generating profits that are likely to cover other, fixed costs in the business.  So this acquisition strategy is sound.   (1 mark for conclusion)

Answer text Question 4
Feedback

Intent-based channels are where users are to be found who are searching with Intent to buy or otherwise complete an action. In Awareness-based channels users are present without Intent.   (1 mark). Awareness-based channels are still a useful way to build brand awareness and to reduce the likelihood that users will forget about the business.  (1 mark)

Question text

Explain what is meant by the Inertial Phase of a Compounding Growth mechanism.  Provide one example from each of two separate businesses (i.e two examples in total), of approaches to overcome this inertia.       
Answer text Question 6
Feedback

At the start of a CGM’s lifecycle, it is often more difficult to encourage users either to refer other users and for users to act on invitations to sign-up.   (1 mark).  

One strategy to overcome this inertia is to initially operate the CGM on an unprofitable basis, through advertising spend or subsidy, to increase user numbers operating within the mechanism (for example, Uber employed this technique. Another is to concentrate budgets and team attention on one area, for example, one city (for example, scooter start-ups often take this approach).  

(Each of these examples will gain a one mark, as will similar examples, provided that they are based on the techniques of temporary non-profitability, or concentration of effort in a small area).

Question text

A growth team constructs a Quantitative Growth Model for its product, an n-sided network based trading platform.   Explain what future trend you would incorporate into the quantitative model for linear acquisition, for this product.
Answer text Question 8
Feedback

This is a moving variable question.  We would expect the variable to move as follows:

1) As network effects become stronger, linear acquisition performance would start to improve due to these network effects - it becomes easier to attract new users.   (1 mark)

2) Eventually, as the market begins to saturate, acquisition performance would stabilise, and then drop over time. (1 mark)

Question text

A founder-CEO of a rapidly growing technology start-up laments to her co-founders that engineers don't release features as quickly as was the case when she and her co-founders were doing all of the work, and that everything just seems to take longer now.    She concludes that the problem is that the quality of people the company is hiring must be the issue.    Comment on her conclusion and suggest what action she should take first.
Answer text Question 10
Feedback

The CEO may be correct. But it is more likely that there is a system-level problem in the business, caused by organizational growth.   The CEO should first assess how much agency the front-line staff operate under.  Specifically:

    Everyone is clear what their job is and what decisions they own
    They are competent to do this job, recognising that competence decreases with business growth, unless reinvested in.
    They are well aligned to the strategy and goals of the business.
    They have good access to the skills and resources they need to carry out tasks.

She should next check the performance of key processes in the business, and whether they have degraded.

If all of the above checks out well, she can consider staff capability further - it is possible that some staff have just "scaled-out" and can't operate well in a larger business.

(Marking: 1 mark for considering agency. 1 mark for exploring each part of agency, 1 mark for general quality and depth of answer).
