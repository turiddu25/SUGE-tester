        Introduction and overview

        Welcome to Start-up Growth Engineering, a course running in the second semester.

        Class Schedule

        Classes are held on Tuesdays from 3:00pm to 5:00pm and are in-person, interactive lectures, held in the Gilbert Scott Building ("Main Building") Room 466.   

        This room is located on the 4th Floor of the "West Quadrangle - South" (good to know, if you happen to carry a magnetic compass with you on campus, or own a homing pigeon.). However you do it, I recommend that you locate this room prior to our first lecture, so that you can arrive in good time.

        Classes will be audio-recorded and posted on this page within 24 hours of the lecture ending.   Slides used in the class will also be available  in the relevant weekly section on the morning of the class.  Questions and discussion during the lectures are welcome and encouraged.

        Tutorial Schedule

        These are held on Fridays from 1:00pm to 2:00pm, are in-person events and are held in the JMS Learning Hub, Room 743.

        Here's how the tutorials work:
            The tutorials give you a forum to discuss course content and ask questions about it.   
            We'll recap the main points from the lecture and review recent quiz questions.
            There will also be several presentations on career advice throughout the term.  These discussions not examinable content, but are intended to help you after graduation.   Students in previous years have found these to be very useful.
            It's much appreciated if you can submit your questions to me before the lab (mark.logan@glasgow.ac.uk) or on the Start-up Growth Engineering Team in MS Teams, so that I can collate an agenda before the tutorial.   But you can, of course, still bring additional questions and points on the day.  You are also welcome to listen in on the session, even if you don't have a specific question.   If you have a question that you would like to be treated anonymously, please say so when you submit it to me by email.
            Remember that tutorials are not optional.  Students who skip them usually perform a lot worse on the course than students who attend.

        Coursework

        The coursework for this class consists of three quizzes and one large assignment.  Quizzes will be posted on this page in Weeks 2, 4 and 6, shortly after the Tuesday class.  The quizzes cover the course material for the preceding recent classes (i.e. Week 1+2 Quiz, Week 3+4 Quiz and Week 5, 6, 7 Quiz).   

        The Main Assignment will be posted on this page in Week 7. 

        All quizzes and the assignment will be posted under the relevant course weekly section on this page (see below).

        You'll also be notified on MS Teams also when the assignment has been posted.    You have 2 weeks from the date of issue to complete and submit each quiz. For the main assignment, you have from the Tuesday of Week 7 until the last day of to complete it (approximately 4 weeks).  All quizzes and the assignment will be marked and, together, count for 50% of your overall grade, structured as follows:

        Week 1+2 Quiz          5%

        Week 3+4 Quiz        10%

        Week 5, 6, 7 Quiz     10%

        Main Assignment   25%

        If you need to request an extension or apply for Extenuating Circumstances, you can find more information and the support available here.

        Revision Resources

        Every lecture has an accompanying written revision guide/lecture summary. These are published each Friday of the term onto this Moodle page, next to the lecture slides.   These are useful during revision and exam preparation, and also if you miss a lecture for some reason.  They are not a general substitute for the lecture material, though.

        Prior to the exam, and sometime after the end of the 10-week teaching term,  I will also hold a revision tutorial.

        This week's lab: Steve Jobs Answer to OpenDoc question URL

        At this week's lab, we discussed how the most effective engineers are "T-shaped", which is to say that they are relatively deeply knowledgable in some areas but also strive to develop a broad knowledge across adjacent areas.  This video is an answer by Steve Jobs to a question about why Apple decided to abandon the OpenDoc document format.  At this time, this powerful framework was supposed to become the new standard for document exchange, and a Microsoft killer (which was completely dominant in the industry at that time).   But, when Jobs returned to Apple, he extracted Apple from the programme.  It's very instructive to listen to his reason for doing so and to see the difference between T-shaped Jobs and the I-shaped questioner.

        This section contains a various study resources to help you to prepare for the examination.

        1) A "crib sheet" to help you prepare for the upcoming exam. 

        2) A link to the Week 10 Revision lecture
