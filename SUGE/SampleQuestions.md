Describe the three main types of Acquisition channels.
Answer text Question 1
Feedback

The three types are:

    Intent-based channels are where users are to be found who are searching with Intent to buy or otherwise complete an action.   (1 mark). 
    Awareness-based channels are where users do not have intent at the point where ads are presented to them. (1 mark)
    Referral-based mechanisms act as an additional type of channel, bringing new users who have accepted invitiations from other users, or been indirectly referred to the product. (1 mark)

Question text

Explain why businesses aim to achieve an LTV:CAC ratio of at least 3:1.
Answer text Question 5
Feedback

If CAC > LTV then the acquisition activity is losing the business money on each customer acquisition, which is clearly not sustainable. 

It is not sufficient for LTV to be simply the same as or larger than CAC, because the business has many other overheads and fixed costs. Therefore a ratio of 3:1 or above generally enables a business to be profitable, even accounting for these costs.

Explain how Growth teams use the Activation Inequality to optimise product activation performance. 
Answer text Question 6
Feedback

The Activation Inequality asserts that the value perceived by the user must be greater than the effort perceived to realise that value, otherwise the user will be lost before Stage 1 of Activation is completed. (1 mark).   

Growth and design teams can use the concept when considering, for example, the ordering of steps in the activation process and the amount of information requested from users before delivering value to them. (1 mark).

Question text

Explain the composition (i.e. the structure) of a retention metric.    How do Growth teams decide which specific retention metric to use?
Answer text Question 9
Feedback

The retention metric quantifies the intensity and frequency of an action that correlates most strongly with future user retention. It has the form NaP where

N = number of times an action is performed

a = the name of the action

P = the period in which it is performed.

(2 marks for the above points)

 

Start-ups can set a retention metric by examining how actions at given frequencies and intensities correlate with future retention of users. The stronger the correlation, the better the candidate metric is likely to be.    (1 mark)

In the earlier stages of a start-up, where either there isn't enough user traffic or where there hasn't passed a sufficient period to carry out the correlation, Start-ups can choose a reasonable candidate metric, and then refine that choice over time.   (1 mark)

Explain why Growth teams sometimes use two different content-based Indirect CGM subtypes in sequence, as part of a growth strategy.
Answer text Question 12
Feedback

There are two main sub-types of Content-based indirect compounding growth mechanism; Company-generated content-based (CG), and User-generated content-based (UG).  (1 mark)

The first of these types is easier to move through the Inertial stage but is harder to scale.  The second type has the opposite characteristics. Therefore, it sometimes make sense to combine them in sequence: start with a CG Content mechanism and then add a UG Content mechanism. (1 mark)

Explain how a start-up should decide which sub-type of Direct Compounding Growth Mechanism is likely to be most effective for its product.
Answer text Question 13
Feedback

There are three sub-types:  Personal Utility-based, Financial and Social-capital-based.    A start-up in which users experience significant benefit from the referral, in terms of subsequent product value will prefer a Personal-Utility model.  The Financial sub-type is better when no such "selfish benefit" exists.  Social Capital effects are often an additional aspect of these first two mechanisms, but can also drive referral when no direct benefit to users in referring exists, and where the Business Model of the start-up can't sustain a financial mechanism.   (3 marks for this or similar answer.)

Explain the operation of an n-sided network.     Give an example of a well-known product based on an n-sided network, as part of your answer.                 
Answer text Question 16
Feedback

In general Network Effects, the greater the participation in network, the greater the value the network has to its participants.  This results in more participants joining the network which, in turn, increases its value, which attracts still further participants.     (1 mark for explaining the basic concept)

In an n-Sided network, there are n types of participant.  In this configuration, one type of participants acts as an attractor for other types.  (1 mark)

For example, Facebook consists of users, advertisers and content providers.  The presence of users attract content providers. The content from those providers attracts additional users.   (1 mark)

Explain how start-ups attempt to manage liquidity in the earliest stages of a 2-sided network's lifetime.   
Answer text Question 17
Feedback

Liquidity in a 2-sided network concerns ensuring that there is sufficient supply and sufficient demand, and that the supply and demand are balanced.  In an early stage network, there is usually a lack of both supply and demand (1 mark for explaining the basic problem).

Start-ups can deploy temporary measures to increase supply, such as incentivising suppliers to join the network or compensating them for lack of earnings. (1 mark for two examples similar to these - there are multiple valid examples). 

For the demand side, techniques include driving referral mechanisms with incentives, unsustainable advertising costs and discounting when using the service. (1 mark for two examples similar to these - there are multiple valid examples).

Explain how, in a 2-sided network,  networks effects can strengthen the growth performance of compounding growth mechanisms at the demand-side of the network.
Answer text Question 18
Feedback

The effect of the 2-sided network is to attract more users to the demand-side. This happens because they are attracted by the available supply on the other side of the network.  (1 mark).  

This effect means that the demand-side compounding growth mechanism is acting upon a greater number of users in each iteration, generating more returns in each iteration. (1 mark)  The network attraction also creates more impetus for referred users to join the product (1 mark)

Question text

Explain, with reference to any applicable principles, why it is very difficult for an organization that is highly focussed on its internal functional hierarchy to successfully optimize the Growth Model's Customer Journey.
Answer text Question 20
Feedback

The Customer Journey requires multiple different skillsets to work closely together.   It also requires that the organization is focussed upon this process in a continuous and sustained manner.  This is more difficult, if the organization is designed around a structure that separates people according to their skillset, and does not routinely combine people from different skill-areas of the business into teams. (2 marks)

Conway's law describes how organizations tend to mirror their organizational structures in the products that they build. (1 mark for this or similar definition).

In the present case, Conway's law predicts that the reliance on a purely functional hierarchy will put interfaces between teams that disrupt the requirement to work as a single team on the Customer Journey. (1 mark for this or similar description).

Explain the differences between the investment stages Seed, Series A and Series B.
Answer text Question 24
Feedback

These investment stages relate to different stages in the growth of a start-up.  Seed capital is typically < $2million, corresponding to the stage where the start-up is seeking product-market fit.   Series A is typically awarded to drive growth and is of the order of $10million.  Series B ($30m+) and later stages are typically correspondingly larger, to further drive growth.
